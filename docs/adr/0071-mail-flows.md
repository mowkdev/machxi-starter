# ADR-0071: Mail flows — verified addresses, recovery, invitations delivered, addresses changed (supersedes ADR-0017, 0018, 0027 and 0057 in part — deltas)

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-09-10
- Supersedes: [ADR-0018](0018-authentication-sessions-and-tokens.md) (delta: self-registration creates a `pending_verification` account and issues no session — the verification link does; every other decision about sessions and tokens stands); [ADR-0017](0017-identity-model-populations-and-roles.md) (delta: `users` gains `email_verified_at`, `staff.password_hash` becomes nullable so an `invited` operator has no password rather than a placeholder, and the two statuses that record already names — `pending_verification`, `invited` — acquire the flows that clear them; both populations, their tables, their roles and every invariant stand); [ADR-0027](0027-credential-self-service-and-reset.md) (delta: the reset this record left out is built, the profile gains a change of address with confirmation, and the administrative operations gain a change of address for a super admin; the current-password rule, the session rule and the administrative reset stand); [ADR-0057](0057-impersonation-sessions.md) (delta: §5's exclusion list grows the address change — its fourth partial successor; everything else stands)

> Written 2026-09-10 by `/scope` for D18 ([`docs/plan/0018-mail-flows.md`](../plan/0018-mail-flows.md)) — writing is not opening, opening is not accepting. `proposed` at writing; **accepted at the head of `D18.1`**, where the four parents move at the step that makes each delta true (the `D10.5` precedent; the plan names which). Amendable in place, labelled, while D18 is `in progress` ([the ledger](../plan/README.md#the-window)).

## Context

[ADR-0066](0066-infrastructure-seams.md) §1 shipped a `MailSender` port with two adapters and Mailpit in the local compose, and said in so many words that the flows are not that record: "password reset, address verification, email change and invitations each need a token table, limiters and their own decisions". Three records have been waiting on those decisions by name. [ADR-0027](0027-credential-self-service-and-reset.md) left "forgot my password" unbuilt because it needs delivery, a single-use token table and the `3 / hour` limiter [ADR-0020](0020-security-baseline.md) reserved a row for. [ADR-0067](0067-organizations.md) §2 mints an invitation's `token_hash` that nothing reads, and its §Consequences records the exposure that address-match acceptance carries while no address is verified — the finding `D15.1` escalated and the operator answered with the token column rather than the gate, on the ground that the gate "is an email flow, D15 ships none". [ADR-0017](0017-identity-model-populations-and-roles.md) gave users a `pending_verification` status and staff an `invited` one, and the auth principals code refuses both at login with a comment that says the flow which clears them is future.

The operator's decision of 2026-09-10 was that Google sign-in ([D19](../plan/0019-google-sign-in.md)) waits for this: a federated identity may only be attached to a local account whose address is verified, and no address is. So this record decides the flows, and it decides them as one deliverable because they share one mechanism — a single-use token, hashed at rest, delivered in a link, consumed once inside the transaction that acts on it — which [ADR-0057](0057-impersonation-sessions.md) §2 already built for the impersonation handoff and which every flow here would otherwise rebuild.

## Decision

### 1. One token table, one mechanism

`account_tokens`: `id`, `purpose` (`verify_email | reset_password | change_email | staff_onboarding`), `principal_type`, `principal_id` (the `sessions` discriminator shape of ADR-0017), `token_hash` (unique; SHA-256 of a `randomToken(32)`, stored only as the hash — the `sessions.refresh_token_hash` form), `payload jsonb` (empty for most purposes; the new address for `change_email`), `expires_at`, `consumed_at`, `ip`, `user_agent`. The token is **strictly single-use**: consumption is `update … where consumed_at is null` returning, inside the consuming transaction, and a refused consumption rolls the stamp back — exactly ADR-0057 §2 and §3's rules, restated here because they are the property every flow below relies on. Minting a token for a purpose **supersedes the live one** for the same principal and purpose (the older row is consumed on the spot), so a person who clicks "resend" holds one valid link, not a growing set. Consumed and expired rows are inert and `db:sessions:prune` grows their deletion, as it did for handoffs.

The organization invitation keeps its own table and its own `token_hash` — ADR-0067 §2 shaped it and it carries organization state a generic row does not — and this record makes it readable (§5).

Every link carries its token in the **URL fragment** (`#token=…`) and lands on a route of the SPA that strikes the fragment from history and posts the token to the API: 0057 §2's argument, inherited whole — no server log, proxy or `Referer` sees it — and 0057's `D13.7` amendment about the profile's history copy, also inherited: the mitigation is the TTL, and each purpose has its own (`VERIFICATION_TOKEN_TTL` 24 h, `PASSWORD_RESET_TOKEN_TTL` 1 h, `EMAIL_CHANGE_TOKEN_TTL` 1 h, `STAFF_ONBOARDING_TOKEN_TTL` 7 d; the invitation keeps `ORGANIZATION_INVITATION_TTL`). The API builds the links, so it learns the two SPA origins: `CUSTOMER_APP_URL` and `ADMIN_APP_URL` enter ADR-0004's schema, defaulting to the local ports and required in production, beside the `VITE_` twins the SPAs already read.

### 2. Templates and their language

Templates are plain functions on the `MailTemplate` shape, one per flow, in the API's mail core; each takes a `locale` and ships **English only**. Where an account exists its stored locale ([ADR-0046](0046-interface-localization.md): a locale is an account fact) is passed; where none does — registration, an invitation to a stranger — the SPA's current locale travels in the request body. The Latvian dictionary is one later step with no signature change, which is what ADR-0066 §1 asked "the first flow that needs a second language" to decide.

### 3. Address verification — an account is usable when its address is proven

Self-registration (`POST /public/auth/register`) creates the account as **`pending_verification`**, mails the link, and answers **`202` with no session** — ADR-0018's "registration creates `active`" is the delta. `POST /public/auth/verify-email { token }` consumes the token, sets `status = active` and `email_verified_at = now()`, and answers **login's body on cookie transport**, the impersonation exchange's shape: the person has just proven the address, and the device the link was opened on is signed in. Login on a `pending_verification` account with the **right** password answers `403 AUTH_EMAIL_UNVERIFIED` — distinguishable, because the caller has already proven the password and ADR-0020 §Enumeration has nothing left to protect; with the wrong one it is the same `AUTH_INVALID_CREDENTIALS` as ever. `POST /public/auth/resend-verification { email }` always answers `202`, never an oracle, behind its own limiter keyed on address and email.

**The invariant this buys:** every `active` user has a proven address — verified by the link, or **asserted** by the staff member who created it through `POST /admin/users` or set it under §7, which stamps `email_verified_at` the same way. Address-match acceptance of an invitation (ADR-0067 §5) therefore closes `D15.1`'s exposure without a new rule, and D19's linking rule reads "active" and nothing else. Accounts that existed before this record are backfilled as verified by migration — they were created under ADR-0018's rule and refusing them would punish a decision that was right when made.

### 4. Password reset — both populations, no session from the link

`POST /{admin,user}/auth/password/forgot { email }` beside each segment's login, unauthenticated, **always `202`** — never an oracle — behind the `3 / hour` per address-and-email limiter ADR-0020 reserved (`RATE_LIMIT_PASSWORD_RESET_MAX`; the window is the limiter's own, one hour, and the rate-limit module grows a per-limiter window for it). A disabled, deleted or `pending_verification` account is mailed nothing and answered the same `202`. `POST /{admin,user}/auth/password/reset { token, newPassword }` consumes the token, sets the hash, **revokes every session of the account** (the person is recovering it from somebody, possibly) and answers `204` **without a session**: the link proved the mailbox, not the device, and signing in is one step away. Both populations get the flow — ADR-0027's "another admin resets you" stays true and stops being the only path — and `super_admin` is no exception, which retires the `pnpm db:seed` recovery path's monopoly without retiring the path.

### 5. Invitations delivered, and accepted by the link

Creating an invitation (`POST /user/orgs/:orgId/invitations`, ADR-0067 §5) **sends** it — the marked call in the organizations service — with a link to the customer app carrying the token ADR-0067 §2 mints. Two reads become real, under the two conditions ADR-0067 §5's amendment stated for them and this record inherits: `POST /public/invitations/lookup { token }` answers the organization's name, the invited address and the role, is rate-limited as an authentication route, and answers one indistinguishable `404` for unknown, consumed, declined and expired; and `POST /user/invitations/accept-by-token { token }`, authenticated, accepts the invitation for the caller **when the caller's address is the invited one** — the token proves the mailbox and the session proves the account, and the two must agree. A stranger who follows the link registers with the address prefilled and locked, verifies (§3), and accepts; an account holder signs in and accepts. Address-match acceptance by id stands as ADR-0067 decided, safe under §3's invariant. Re-sending is revoke-and-create, which the surfaces already have.

### 6. A person changes their own address by proving the new one

`POST /user/profile/change-email { newEmail, currentPassword }` — the current password for the reason ADR-0027 requires it on a password change — refuses an address a live account already holds with `USER_EMAIL_TAKEN`, mints a `change_email` token whose payload is the new address, and mails **the new address**; nothing changes until `POST /public/auth/confirm-email { token }` consumes it, re-checks uniqueness inside the transaction, swaps the address and stamps `email_verified_at`. The old address is notified after the fact, plainly, with no link. Sessions are untouched — an address is not a credential. **Refused under impersonation** with `AUTH_IMPERSONATION_REFUSED`: a changed address redirects every future recovery link, which is the definition of takeover-enabling, and ADR-0057 §5's list grows by it — its fourth partial successor.

### 7. A super admin sets any account's address

`POST /admin/staff/:id/email` and `POST /admin/users/:id/email`, body `{ email, currentPassword }` — the administrative reset's shape of ADR-0027, the acting operator's own password — behind **`authorize("super_admin")`** at the route, which is the first route to name that role alone (ADR-0019's guard, not a new mechanism; [ADR-0042](0042-client-side-authorization.md)'s policy gains the matching row so the affordance is hidden from everyone else). The address is set immediately and counts as **asserted** (§3): no confirmation link, because the operator is the authority the link would be asking. Uniqueness among live accounts is refused with the population's `*_EMAIL_TAKEN`. A super admin may name their own id; the last-super-admin invariant is untouched because nothing here changes roles or status. Every change — §6's and this one — writes an `account.email_changed` audit row naming actor, subject, old and new address, on ADR-0058's transactional discipline.

### 8. Staff onboarding by invitation

`POST /admin/staff` accepts a body **without a password**: the row is created `invited` with a `null` hash (the 0017 delta), an onboarding token is minted and mailed with a link to the **admin** app, and the row is unusable until `POST /admin/auth/onboarding/accept { token, password }` consumes the token, sets the hash and `active`, and answers login's body on cookie transport. A staff member created **with** a password is `active` at once, exactly as today. `POST /admin/staff/:id/onboarding/resend` (admin) re-mints for an `invited` row and nothing else. Login on an `invited` row is refused as any inactive row is.

### 9. What stands

Sessions, rotation, reuse detection, the cookie transport and the CSRF discipline; the mint and every other rule of impersonation; the invitation's shape, its address-match acceptance and its enumeration-safe creation; the current-password rule wherever a password changes; the `MailSender` port and both adapters.

## Consequences

- Every flow is a token row, a template, a public consume endpoint and an SPA route that strikes a fragment — one shape learned once. The cost is the size of the deliverable that learns it; the plan is twelve steps.
- The browser tier reads its links from Mailpit's HTTP API, so `pnpm test:e2e` runs with `MAIL_TRANSPORT=smtp` against the local compose, and the nightly workflow gains Mailpit as a service beside Postgres. A tier that cannot read the mail cannot prove a single flow here.
- Four more limiters' worth of budget accounting in the tier's window floors; `D18.12` re-measures.
- The dev seeds mark their fixtures verified, or the fixtures cannot sign in.
- `docs/specs/` gains a `mail-flows.md` for the token mechanism, verification, reset, address change and onboarding; invitations' delivery joins `organizations.md`; registration's new answer moves in `auth-sessions.md`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Verified after login.** Registration keeps issuing a session, the account is active with an empty `email_verified_at`, and a banner nags. Friendlier for the first minute, and every later flow — invitations, D19's linking, recovery — re-checks a flag instead of leaning on an invariant. Rejected at the operator's decision.
- **One table per purpose.** Four tables with the same five columns and four prune predicates. The `purpose` column is cheaper and the payload column carries the one flow that needs more.
- **The token in a query string** — a link that survives being pasted into anything. Rejected for the reason 0057 §2 rejected it: a credential in a path is a credential in a log.
- **Reset issues a session.** Common, and one step shorter. Rejected because the link proved a mailbox and revoked every session; handing the very next request a session on whatever device opened the mail undoes half of that.
- **Field-level role checks** — `email` accepted on `PATCH /admin/{staff,users}/:id` only for a super admin. A route-level guard is what ADR-0019 gives and what the client policy can mirror; a body field whose acceptance depends on the caller's role is a second authorization mechanism nobody asked for.
