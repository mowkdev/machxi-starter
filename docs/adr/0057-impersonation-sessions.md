## Context

Staff must be able to open the customer app **as a chosen customer**: see what they see, fix what they cannot describe, and leave a trail. The pieces have been waiting for it by name — the shell's `banner` slot and the `Banner` composition's `warning` variant (ADR-0048, `banner.tsx`), ADR-0055's rejection of a mode-of-admin partly *because* impersonation needs two apps, and ADR-0056, whose per-population CSRF cookies mean two sessions in one browser no longer share mutable transport state. What does not exist: any way to mint a `user` session other than that user's password, any attribution of a session to a second identity, and (per [ADR-0058](0058-append-only-audit-log.md)'s context) any audit mechanism. The hard problem is the handoff: sessions live in httpOnly cookies on the API host, and minting must neither touch the staff session that asked nor silently destroy a real customer session server-side.

Four decisions were taken by the operator at scoping (2026-09-01) and are carried here: audit goes to an **append-only table** (0058), not logs alone; a pre-existing customer session in the minting browser is **overwritten in the cookie jar and left alive in the database**; **settings writes are allowed** under impersonation, audited; and the standing assumption — full access minus credential operations — is confirmed.

## Decision

### 1. The mint — `POST /api/v1/admin/users/:id/impersonate`

Staff-guarded on the admin segment, `authorize("admin")` — a `manager` is neither offered ([ADR-0042](0042-client-side-authorization.md)'s table) nor allowed, and `super_admin` passes by ADR-0019's one line. The target must be an active, non-deleted user; anything else is refused before a token exists. The endpoint gets **its own rate limiter keyed on the staff principal's id** (`RATE_LIMIT_IMPERSONATION_MAX`, default 10 per window): ADR-0020's login limiter keys on `IP + email` read from the body, which a mint does not carry, and an authenticated high-value operation wants a per-actor budget, not a per-IP one. Because its key is the principal, this limiter mounts **after** the admin segment's `authenticate`, inside the segment — a named refinement of ADR-0011's rate-limit-first order, which holds for the IP-keyed limiters and cannot hold for one that reads `ctx.principal`.

The response carries a **handoff token, not a session**: no `Set-Cookie` of any kind, which is what leaves the staff session untouched — the admin segment's cookies are never written by this flow at all (ADR-0056's "each segment touches only its own", inherited rather than re-decided).

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.4`).** The paragraph above fixes the limiter after `authenticate` and says nothing about `authorize`, and the route has to put it on one side or the other. It goes **after** the role guard: the key is the staff principal's id, so a refused `manager` spends nothing, and the budget bounds the operators who can actually mint rather than everyone who can reach the URL. That is a departure from the login limiter, whose "count an attempt that never reached a schema" property exists because an anonymous caller could otherwise get unlimited guesses — there is nothing to guess here, the caller is already authenticated, and `authorize` is one array comparison rather than an argon2 hash. The refusals the target check produces are likewise *distinguishable* (`404` for an unknown account, `409 USER_DELETED` for a deleted one, plain `409 CONFLICT` for an inactive one) where §3's are not: the caller is authenticated staff who may already list every customer and read their status, so there is no oracle to close, and an operator told "that account is disabled" does not report a broken button.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.8`), where that last clause first met an operator.** The three refusals are distinguishable *on the wire*, and only two of them are distinguishable *on the screen*. `apps/admin` renders the `errors` catalogue's sentence for a code and never the API's `error.message` ([ADR-0046](0046-interface-localization.md)) — so `NOT_FOUND` and `USER_DELETED` each have a sentence written for them and name the reason, while a bare `CONFLICT` has the catalogue's generic one, which is about something already existing and is not what the sentence above promises anyone. (The sentences themselves stay in the catalogue and out of this record: they are copy a test owns, and quoting them here would make a rewrite silently falsify an ADR.) The code is still right by [ADR-0013](0013-error-model.md): nothing branches on it, and `AUTH_ACCOUNT_DISABLED` would be a lie about whose account was refused. What is wrong is this record claiming an operator learns the reason. Two ways out — a domain code (`@repo/contracts`, the API, both apps' catalogues, and an already-built step reopened) or leaving the generic sentence beside a Status column that already says `Pending verification` — and choosing between them decides what this deliverable contains, which is settled at `/scope` and not inside a build step ([ADR-0050](0050-adr-lifecycle-and-verification.md)'s loop), so `D13.8` recorded the gap instead of closing it. Until it is closed, the argument for distinguishable refusals stands on the HTTP status alone.

### 2. The handoff token

Opaque (`randomToken(32)`), stored **only as its SHA-256 hash** in a new `impersonation_handoffs` table (token hash unique, staff id, target user id, expiry, `consumed_at`, mint ip/ua), short-lived (`IMPERSONATION_HANDOFF_TTL`, default 60s), and **strictly single-use**: the exchange consumes it atomically (`update … where consumed_at is null` returning), and a replay — inside any window — answers `401` with nothing minted. This is why the handoff is not simply the session's refresh token delivered by URL: ADR-0018's rotation grace deliberately tolerates a replayed refresh for 30 seconds, and a credential that travels through a URL must not have a grace window. The admin app delivers it to the customer app in the **URL fragment** (`/impersonate#token=…`), which no server log or `Referer` sees. Consumed and expired handoff rows are inert; the `db:sessions:prune` sweep (ADR-0018 §Cleanup) grows their deletion.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.7`, out of `security-reviewer`'s residual reading): the fragment's claim is about the network, and there is one local copy it does not cover.** "No server log or `Referer` sees it" is exactly true and was read, at the step that built the screen, as though it meant the token lands nowhere durable. It lands in one place: the browser writes the fragment-bearing URL into the profile's own history at load, before any script runs, and `history.replaceState` does not retract that entry. The customer app therefore strikes the fragment out of the *session* history immediately (`replace`, so Back cannot walk to it) — which is the whole of what a client can do — and the profile history keeps a copy of a credential that is single-use and sixty seconds long. That is judged acceptable rather than overlooked: the entry outlives the credential's usefulness by construction, and the two designs that would avoid it (a `POST` form from the admin origin, or a server-side one-time redirect) each put the token somewhere §2 rejected it — a body a `Referer` can accompany, or a path a log records. The mitigation that is real and is taken is the TTL.

### 3. The exchange — `POST /api/v1/user/auth/impersonate`

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.4`):** every way this endpoint can fail answers one indistinguishable **`401 UNAUTHENTICATED`** — unknown token, already consumed, past `IMPERSONATION_HANDOFF_TTL`, target deleted or disabled, minting staff member gone. This record had not named a code, and the alternatives were each wrong for a reason: `AUTH_IMPERSONATION_REFUSED` belongs to §5's exclusion list, where it is a `403` on a live session; a fourth handoff-shaped code would be enum growth whose only reader — the customer app's `/impersonate` screen — has one sentence to say either way, and would need both apps' catalogues to grow for it. The generic 401-family code is what ADR-0013 offers for a refusal that must not distinguish its causes, and it is the same posture `AUTH_INVALID_CREDENTIALS` takes towards the other opaque credential in the system (ADR-0020 §Enumeration). One consequence is deliberate: an already-shipped bundle renders it without any change at all.

A refused exchange **does not spend the token**: the consume shares the exchange's transaction, so a refusal after the claim rolls the stamp back and the row stays redeemable for the rest of its sixty seconds. §2's "strictly single-use" is therefore exactly *at most one session per handoff*, which is the property worth having — nothing was minted, so nothing was spent — and the bound on a retried failure is the TTL rather than the check that failed.

The exchange also re-reads **both** accounts rather than trusting the handoff row, and refuses with that same `401` when either is gone — the target because a minute is enough for an account to be disabled, and the minting staff member because §7 makes the banner unremovable and a session whose actor cannot be resolved is one no banner could ever name. That refusal is not the judgement made on *refresh*, where the session already exists and ending it would punish a support operator for somebody else's deletion; there the actor simply stops resolving.

On the user segment's auth router, unauthenticated — the token is the credential. The auth router is one factory mounted per segment, so the route registers **only when the factory's population is `user`** — an unconditional registration would create an admin-segment twin. It consumes the handoff, creates the session (§4), sets the user-population cookies **exactly as login does — on ADR-0029's transport opt-in (`X-Auth-Transport`), never unconditionally** — the path-scoped refresh cookie and `__Host-machxi.csrf.user` — and returns the login-shaped `AuthResource`. If the browser already held a real customer session, its cookies are overwritten and **its session row is not touched**: the customer's other devices are unaffected, and this browser signs back in normally afterwards (the operator's call; refusing would break every staff member who is also a customer, and revoking would end a session that was not the actor's to end).

### 4. The session

A `user`-population session with two deltas on ADR-0018, both visible in the row:

- **`sessions.impersonated_by_staff_id`** (uuid, nullable, **deliberately no foreign key**): staff are hard-deleted (ADR-0028), and attribution must survive its actor's deletion.
- **A fixed end.** `refresh_token_expires_at` is set **at the exchange** to now + `IMPERSONATION_SESSION_TTL` (default 30m) and **rotation does not extend it** — the one change to ADR-0018's refresh flow, applied only when the actor column is set. Refresh otherwise works normally (rotation, reuse detection, the grace window), which is what lets a reload survive: the app boots off the cookie as ever. When the end passes, refresh answers `401` and the app signs out. An impersonation that needs more time is **re-minted**, on the trail, never extended. The access token's `exp` is capped at the session's end, so no JWT outlives it.

The JWT gains an actor claim (RFC 8693's `act` shape, carrying the staff id) so `authenticate` can put `impersonatedBy` on `ctx.principal` without a second lookup; the session row remains the source of truth at refresh. The wire principal — `UserPrincipalResource` in `@repo/contracts` — gains an optional **`impersonatedBy: { id, email }`**, resolved from the staff table at the auth endpoints only (exchange, refresh, me), never per request. **Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.4`): `me` is struck from that list.** It was written as three endpoints for symmetry rather than for a reader, and there is none: neither SPA calls `/auth/me` — both boot off `refresh` (ADR-0033), which carries the field on the same response — and `me` exists to resynchronise *after* one. Resolving it there would buy a second primary-key probe on every `me` of **both** populations, ordinary sessions included, for a client that has already been told. The email is why this had to be decided rather than deferred: the `act` claim carries an id and no address, so `D13.5` putting the actor on `ctx.principal` would not have produced `{ id, email }` here on its own, and the omission would have looked like an oversight instead of a decision. The day something boots off `me`, the resolver is one call away and this sentence is what it argues with. The banner renders from this field and from nothing else: it survives reload because the session does, and it is unforgeable because the API set it.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.6`): the client's caches are keyed on the subject *and* the actor.** Not stated above and not derivable from it, because the field was written for a banner. `GET /user/settings` — and every other `/user` read — resolves its subject from `ctx.principal`, so a customer's own session and an impersonated session of that customer read **the same rows**: keyed on the subject alone, a client cache would be correct today. `D13.6` keys on both anyway, and the reason is that the coincidence is a property of the API's subject resolution rather than of the seam doing the keying. The eviction boundary drops *every* cached resource at a principal change, so "which of these happen to have one subject under both sessions" would be a question every new key reopened, silently, with adoption rather than an error as the failure mode. A change of actor is a change of session; the cost of saying so is a refetch nobody waits on. Two consequences follow and are decided here: a client rebuilding the principal from a resource that does not carry this field — `PATCH /user/profile` is reachable under impersonation and answers a `UserResource` — **must carry the actor through**, since a profile write cannot change who is acting and dropping it would both retire the banner mid-session and read a settled session as a new principal; and `impersonatedBy` may be absent but is never `null` on the wire, which is what lets the key be a plain optional read.

### 5. The exclusions

An impersonated session is refused the operations that would let an impersonator take the account over, with **one error code for the class, `AUTH_IMPERSONATION_REFUSED` (403)** — the scope sketch said "their own error codes" per operation, and this record decides one: the client already knows which dialog the refusal came from, and a code per operation is enum growth with no reader. The list:

- `POST /user/profile/change-password`
- `DELETE /user/profile` (self-deletion, ADR-0055 §4)
- `POST /user/auth/logout-all` (revoking the customer's real sessions)

Enforced by a **`refuseImpersonated` middleware named in the router files**, ADR-0019's declarative style — never a hidden button. The code enters **both apps'** `errors` catalogues (`en`, `lv`) in the step that widens the enum: each app's compile-time completeness check over `ErrorCode` refuses to build without the sentence, so the catalogue entries cannot trail the code. Everything else the customer can do, the impersonator can do, audited: profile `PATCH`, settings writes (theme, locale, density, grid preferences — the operator's call: reproducing an issue sometimes needs them, and every write lands with both identities). `POST /user/auth/logout` is deliberately **not** excluded — it is the exit.

### 6. Exit, expiry, attribution

The banner's exit control calls the ordinary logout; the service sees the actor column and revokes with reason **`impersonation_ended`**, writing the audit event.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.4`, out of `security-reviewer`'s finding 1): revoking a staff member's sessions must end the sessions they are impersonating through.** Every revocation path is `revokeAllForPrincipal({ principalType, principalId })`, and an impersonated row is `principal_type = 'user'` with the staff id in `impersonated_by_staff_id` — so disabling an operator, deleting them, or pressing the forced-logout button leaves their impersonated session live and rotating for the remainder of `IMPERSONATION_SESSION_TTL`. That is the one case §3's "there the actor simply stops resolving" does not cover: not punishing an operator for somebody else's deletion is a different question from ending the sessions of an operator revoked *for cause*, and ADR-0018's "disabled account, sessions revoked in the same transaction" would otherwise have acquired a class of session it does not reach. A second revocation predicate — `where impersonated_by_staff_id = $1 and revoked_at is null`, reason `impersonation_ended` — runs beside the existing one on the staff disable, delete and forced-logout paths. It is built at **`D13.5`**, which opens that revoke path for the exit control anyway; it is recorded here because the window is open now and the sentence is a decision, not a task. **Each ended session gets its own `impersonation.ended` audit row, inside the revoking transaction** — also `D13.5`, out of that step’s `security-reviewer` pass. The paragraph above ends sessions and said nothing about recording it, and the omission would have been the wrong half: the exit control produced a complete `minted → exchanged → ended` trail while an operator revoked *for cause* — the case this sweep exists for, and the one an incident actually opens the table for — produced silence, with the prunable free-text `sessions.revoked_reason` as its only trace. That is precisely the near-audit [ADR-0058](0058-append-only-audit-log.md) §Context was written to replace. The row carries the revocation’s own reason in `data`, which is what separates “the operator left” from “the operator was removed”. **Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.5`), the step that built it: the sweep runs on _every_ staff revoke-all path, not only the three named above.** The paragraph enumerated the three paths that were known to matter, and the build found that enumerating them is what would go wrong: `revokeAllForPrincipal` is called from six places in `auth.service.ts` alone, a seventh arrives with the next deliverable that ends an account, and a list in a record cannot make the seventh remember. Both predicates therefore live in one private helper that every revoke-all path of the service goes through, and the sweep is conditioned on the population rather than on the caller. Five paths beyond the three named are reached by it and all five are wanted: a staff member’s own `logout-all`, where “end my sessions everywhere” plainly includes the ones they are driving; the revocation `refresh` and `me` perform when they discover a disabled or deleted account; a role change; an administrative password reset of the operator; and the operator’s own password change. The last is why `exceptSessionId` matters below — it is the only one of the eight that spares anything. `exceptSessionId` is deliberately not carried into the second predicate — it spares the caller’s own *staff* session, and an impersonated session is a different row of a different population that the caller is not on. Expiry needs no event of its own: the `exchanged` event records the session's end (the end is anchored at the exchange, §4, so the mint event cannot know it), and an un-exited trail is therefore complete by construction. Every request on an impersonated session carries both identities in the logs — `authenticate`'s `logger.child` and the completion line's `principalProps` gain `impersonatedBy` — and the audit table (0058) receives the lifecycle events (minted, exchanged, ended) plus one row per **mutating** request served under impersonation. Reads are logged, not audited.

### 7. The two clients

- **`apps/admin`**: an Impersonate row action on the users grid; `RESOURCE_POLICY.users` gains `impersonate: ["admin"]` (role-only category — exactly what ADR-0042 permits `can()` to answer), the fixture test grows the row, and the action opens `VITE_CUSTOMER_APP_URL` (new env var, ADR-0004 discipline) at `/impersonate#token=…`.
- **`apps/customer`**: an `/impersonate` route redeems the fragment through **`redeemHandoff(token)`, a new `@repo/api-client` function beside `login`** — the store keeps its single writer set, per that package's own rule, and `COOKIE_TRANSPORT_PATHS` gains `/auth/impersonate` so the exchange opts into cookie transport (§3; without it the session has no cookies and dies on first reload) — then navigates home. `ImpersonationBanner` is a thin per-app component reading `principal.impersonatedBy` into the shell's `banner` slot (`variant="warning"`, `dismissible={false}` — the defaults `banner.tsx` shipped for this by name); exit calls logout and lands on a signed-out screen. Refusals render as catalogue copy like any other code, over the entries §5 places with the enum.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.7`, the step that built it), two things this record left to be inferred and one it got backwards.**

- **The `/impersonate` route sits under neither layout route, and it has to.** This paragraph says where the screen lands and not what guards it, and both of the app's layout guards are wrong for it in opposite directions: `_dashboard`'s would bounce a visitor with no session to `/login` before the exchange could run — carrying a `redirect` and losing the fragment, which never reaches a route's search params anyway — and `_auth`'s mirror guard sends an *authenticated* visitor home, which is precisely the operator §3 describes as already holding a real customer session in this browser. So the exchange screen is guard-free by construction and wears the sign-in shell itself rather than inheriting it. This is written down because it is the one decision in that screen a later reader could undo by tidying the route under `_auth`, and the tidy would look like a simplification.
- **`dismissible={false}` is not written, and should not be.** [ADR-0048](0048-app-shell-and-record-surfaces.md) §3 as amended at `D8.2` made `false` the *default* on exactly this argument — the fail-safe direction is a notice that stays, and this banner is the case it named — so passing the value would be a line that only looks like a decision, and one a later edit could flip without the record noticing. The banner takes the default; the property is the component's.
- **The banner's exit control is a `Banner` prop, not a second element beside it.** §3 of ADR-0048 left an `action` slot out of the v1 API with "one prop wide when a screen names it", and this screen is the one that names it; the prop is a `ReactNode` the app builds, so the composition still does not know the node is a button or what pressing it ends.

**Amended 2026-09-01 (`D13.8`, the step that built the admin half), three things the first bullet left open.**

- **"Opens `VITE_CUSTOMER_APP_URL` at `/impersonate#token=…`" means *this* tab.** The verb was written before anyone had to choose, and both readings are defensible: [ADR-0056](0056-per-population-csrf-cookie.md) makes two sessions in one browser safe, which is exactly what a second tab would rely on. It is still the wrong choice, for a reason that has nothing to do with cookies. A new tab has to be opened *after* the mint answers, because minting before the operator confirms would burn a request against §1's budget and leave a live credential behind a dialog they may cancel — and an open that follows an awaited round trip is the classic popup-blocker case, refused **silently**, leaving the operator with a success, no tab, and a sixty-second credential they can neither spend nor see. A same-tab navigation cannot be refused, needs no window handle to be kept and severed, and leaves no `window.opener` on the customer origin. What it costs is the panel's in-memory state, and the panel is one history entry back with the staff session still live in it — which is what §1's "no `Set-Cookie` of any kind" buys. The call is `window.open(url, "_self")` rather than `location.assign`: the same navigation, and the one form of it that is an ordinary method rather than a `[LegacyUnforgeable]` member no test can observe without a seam that production never runs.
- **`can()` hides this affordance, and it is the first one it hides that ADR-0042 §What it may gate does not name.** That list — navigation entries, the toolbar's Create, the delete entries — was an enumeration of what existed in D4, and 0042 is frozen, so the extension is recorded here rather than there. The *category* is unchanged and is what 0042 actually tests for: the answer depends on `principal.roles` and on nothing else. What is new is the direction. §Containment controls keeps `setPassword`, `revokeSessions` and `update` visible to a `manager` because each is reached for *during* an incident and a hidden control is a silent absence; impersonation inverts both clauses of that argument — no incident is made worse by its absence, and it is the highest-privilege affordance in the panel. So it is hidden, and `RESOURCE_POLICY.users.impersonate` is what hides it. **A third ground was written here and struck (`D13.8`'s `adr-reviewer`): that an offered mint would spend one of §1's per-actor budget for nothing. It would not, and §1's own `D13.4` amendment says why — the limiter mounts *after* `authorize`, precisely so a refused `manager` spends nothing.** The budget argument is real one row down, where the caller is an `admin` whose mint the *service* refuses, and it belongs there rather than here. Where the conjunction happens also moves: ADR-0042 puts it once per screen in `allowedCapabilities`, and this one is in the feature hook instead, because there is no grid capability to narrow — `GridCapabilities` is a platform type in `@repo/resource` that every SPA is built from, and a `canImpersonate` flag there would put one deployment's feature in every app's vocabulary. 0042's reason for narrowing above the grid is that `shared/grid` must not learn a resource's name; a feature hook already knows it.
- **`RESOURCE_POLICY` gains a hole, and the hole is a transcription.** `staff` has no `impersonate` key, because there is no `POST /admin/staff/:id/impersonate` — staff impersonating staff is on this record's own loose-end list. That is the first live case for the `Partial` in `ResourcePolicy`: until now every row granted every action, and the fixture test's completeness check pinned it that way. The check now runs over the six universally-routed actions and the absent seventh is asserted where it belongs — as a fact about the API, beside the `false` the predicate answers for it.

**Not decided here, and named so it is not mistaken for an oversight.** A minted handoff is a bearer credential in a link, and §2 already records that the browser writes the fragment-bearing URL into the profile's history. Nothing in the admin half narrows the window further — no confirmation that the operator, rather than a bystander, is the one who lands on the customer app — because the mint is `authorize("admin")` and the audit table's actor column names whoever asked. Whether "who minted" is a strong enough answer to "who acted" is the same scoping question `D13.7` routed to the operator, and it is still theirs.

### 8. The gate — the ADR-0052 delta

The documented gate grows its fifth link: `pnpm lint && pnpm check-types && pnpm test && pnpm size && pnpm format:check`. Every deliverable since D3 has relied on a once-per-close manual `format:check` sweep for a check a machine can run each step; every document that states the gate changes in the accepting step's sweep, the D10.5 mechanics.

## Consequences

- This is the highest-risk security surface in the ledger, and the plan makes `security-reviewer` mandatory on **every step**, not just the PR gate.
- A new error code meets D12 finding 3: an already-shipped bundle parses `code` as a closed enum and would render `AUTH_IMPERSONATION_REFUSED` as "check your connection". The degradation fix therefore lands at `D13.1`, **before** the code exists — and the property is the one the contracts comment (`errors.ts`) actually states: an unrecognised code degrades to a generic failure **of whatever HTTP status carried it** (an unknown `403` reads as a forbidden-family refusal), never to the transport fallback; the envelope schema is what disagrees with its own file's comment.
- Two new config keys and a limiter key enter ADR-0004's schema and `.env.example`; the e2e suites' `assertApiWindowClear` budget tightens by the new requests. **Amended 2026-09-01 (D13.3):** the sentence said "and `turbo.json`", and none of the three belongs there. ADR-0004's step-7 amendment settled it — `tsdown` performs no `define` substitution, so no variable in the API's schema reaches a build output or turbo's cache key, and declaring one would be a claim the build does not honour; the fifteen keys already in that schema are likewise absent from `turbo.json`. What is declared there is what a task actually reads from the ambient environment (`DATABASE_URL`, `SEED_SUPER_ADMIN_*`, and the `VITE_*` pairs in the two SPA files, which Vite really does inline). `VITE_CUSTOMER_APP_URL` at §7 is one of those and keeps its `turbo.json` entry; these three are not.
- The customer app inherits nothing new in transport: ADR-0056 already made two sessions in one browser safe, which this feature exercises by definition.

## Alternatives considered

- **Read-only impersonation.** Cannot fix anything, which is what support opens the app for; rejected at the 2026-08-20 scoping and confirmed 2026-09-01.
- **Unrestricted impersonation.** A quiet account takeover path; the exclusion list is the difference between a support tool and a liability. Rejected.
- **The refresh token as the handoff.** One mint path and replay-kills-the-session for free — but ADR-0018's grace window would make the URL-borne credential replayable for 30 seconds, and tightening the grace for one session class complicates the hottest auth path. Rejected for a dedicated single-use artifact.
- **A separate cookie namespace for impersonated sessions**, preserving a real customer session's cookies. Every user-segment guard and the CSRF check would need two names and a precedence rule — permanent complexity in the security path to avoid one sign-in after an event that support performs rarely. Rejected.
- **Mint and set cookies in one admin-segment response.** Would have the admin segment writing user-population cookies, precisely the sharing ADR-0056 exists to end. Rejected; the handoff is the design.
