A one-pass, read-only review of `machxi-starter` at commit `630d2b6` (D20 shipped, tag `starter-D20`), eight days after [the 2026-09-13 review](2026-09-13-repo-review.md). It has two halves: **§2 verifies every fate the first review's findings were given at `D20.13`** against the tree rather than against the close-out's notes, and **§3 onward is a fresh pass** over the same areas for what D20 left open or introduced. Ids continue the first review's numbering per area (`API-08`, `SEC-09`, …) so a step can cite either review without ambiguity.

**How it was done.** Six read-only passes, one per area, each re-reading the first review's section, its fate suffixes, the D20 plan and notes, and the records ADR-0073 amended; then the code. Read-only commands: `pnpm lint --filter api`, `pnpm size` (which builds), `pnpm outdated -r`, `pnpm audit --prod`, `pnpm why -r jsdom`, `turbo run … --dry=json`, `pnpm docs:check`, `pnpm test:scripts`, `pnpm rules:digest` (wrote nothing), read-only `psql` against the dev database, and `curl` GETs against the operator's API on `:4000`. Nothing was started, stopped or written outside `docs/reviews/`.

**Severity and effort** as before: `High` fix before the next fork or deployment · `Medium` plan into a deliverable · `Low` opportunistic · `Info` a fact worth knowing; `S` an hour or two · `M` a step · `L` a deliverable.

**Fates, written at `D28.12`** (2026-09-23, the close-out of [D28](../plan/0028-review-follow-ups.md)) — the `D20.13` precedent: every id in §3–§9 carries a bold suffix in its own bullet saying where it went, `Closed at D28.<n>`, the ledger row it was routed to, or that it is an `Info` observation with nothing to close. ## 1. Overall assessment

**D20 did what it said.** All 53 fates written at `D20.13` hold on evidence: 33 findings are closed in the tree, 7 are routed to rows that still describe them, 4 deferrals are still deferred with nothing silently changed, and the 9 `Info` observations are still true. Two closures are honest halves rather than wholes and were recorded as such (`SEC-05`'s scheduler, `FE-04`'s suites); one is half of each of the two fixes it was offered (`UI-02`, below). The closures are mechanised rather than asserted — a hygiene test refuses the buildinfo-only cache, the size ratchet is a tested pure function, the two silent links run through the task graph, every exact pin has a registered reason, Postman now covers all 89 mounted routes — which is why this review found no regression from D20 in any area.

What it found instead is **four Medium gaps of one shape — a control that exists in one place and is missing from its twin:**

1. **`i18n:check` is in `pnpm check` and in no gate link** (`CI-08`) — the same species as `CI-01`, one dependency away from the fix `D20.1` used.
2. **The `jsdom` hold is real for nine workspaces and bypassed for seven** (`STK-08`): `autoInstallPeers` gave vitest's node-environment packages the held `30.1.0`, exactly the "second copy" `renovate.json`'s own note predicts.
3. **ADR-0073's ten decisions have no digest line of their own** (`PRO-08`): `pnpm rules:digest` wrote nothing because nobody extended the generator, so a session loading `docs/rules/` never sees the per-principal budget, the session cap, the headroom rule or the CSP.
4. **Two cooldowns disagree** (`STK-09`): pnpm refuses versions younger than a day, Renovate proposes them on day zero, and nothing says how a security patch crosses that gap.

Beside them, one Medium that is not a gap but a bill: **the admin unit tier is a 3½-minute link and five files are why** (`FE-09`), with v8 coverage instrumented on every run and every worker re-transforming seven source-only packages.

Everything else is `Low` or `Info`: seams the splits left just under the line ceiling, two stale docblocks, a free literal where a union exists, three unindexed prune predicates, a spinner that speaks English in Latvian, nginx as root in the SPA images. **Nothing found is exploitable in the reference deployment.** The structural debt the first review named — the SPA duplication (`FE-01`, re-measured at the same 58 of 112 pairs) and the record chains (now 17 records with two or more successors, the sessions chain at 8) — is unchanged and belongs to D22 and D26 as planned.

## 4. New findings — Security

None is exploitable in the reference deployment; `SEC-09` needs a staff account and lands on the customer.

- **SEC-09 · `Low` · Impersonated sessions count toward the cap and evict the customer's own devices.** `auth.repository.ts:370–400`: `live` includes rows with `impersonated_by_staff_id`; only the _doomed_ set excludes them. A customer on three devices whose account holds seven live impersonated sessions (each lives the impersonation TTL; the mint budget is per staff member) has two real devices signed out at their next login. ADR-0073 §3 decided "count against the impersonated user", so this is the record's consequence rather than a divergence — but it lands on the person least able to explain it. _Fix (S):_ exclude impersonated rows from the count as well, with a one-sentence delta on §3. **Closed at `D28.5`** — excluded from the count as well as the doomed set, the delta on ADR-0073 §3 made by ADR-0074 §4 rather than routed to D24.
- **SEC-10 · `Low` · The policy is untested against the bundle it governs.** The `nginx.conf` files ship only in the images; the browser tier runs against Vite dev servers with no CSP; nothing reads `dist/index.html`. A plugin that emits an inline script breaks the app only in production. _Fix (S):_ a hygiene case after `pnpm size`'s build asserting neither `dist/index.html` contains an inline `<script>`, `<style>` or `on*=` attribute. **Closed at `D28.7`** — inside `pnpm size`'s check on the built `dist/index.html`, not a separate hygiene case.
- **SEC-11 · `Low` · `PRUNE_INTERVAL`'s floor catches only values `sleep` refuses.** `docker/prod/compose.yaml:246`: `0` is valid to `sleep`, so the loop sweeps continuously with `|| true` swallowing every exit and no health check. _Fix (XS):_ a `case` on `''|0|0s` before the loop, or a minimum inside `prune-sessions.ts`. **Closed at `D28.4`** — the compose loop reads the interval once, floored at sixty seconds, anything unreadable a day.
- **SEC-12 · `Info` · `"session_cap"` is a free literal, not a `REVOKE_REASONS` member.** `auth.sessions.service.ts:260`; `RevokeBeyondCapInput.reason` is `string` while every other revoke path is `RevokeReason`. _Fix (XS):_ add the member and type the input. **Closed at `D28.4`.**
- **SEC-13 · `Info` · Stale docblocks after `D20.4`.** `rate-limit.ts:459–466` still says an unvalidated address keys `""` and shares one bucket; `:589` and `rate-limiters.ts:288` say seven mounts (eight exist). _Fix (XS):_ reword. **Closed at `D28.6`.**

**Verified controls, recorded so nobody re-checks.** The `__rate-limit-probe` path is not a route: any unknown `/api/v1/*` URL answers `404 ROUTE_NOT_FOUND` under the global limiter, body carrying only code, message and request id; the draft-8 headers are on every response and `pk=` is a hash of the caller's own key. Timeouts are wired from config before the first accept and the `headers < keep-alive` ordering is safe. Cookies: refresh `httpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict; Path=/api/v1/<segment>/auth`, CSRF `__Host-<app>.csrf.<segment>`, OIDC `__Host-<app>.oidc; httpOnly; Lax`. CSRF coverage is complete by construction — the refresh cookie's path reaches only `/auth`, and every cookie-driven state change there carries the guard; the other user routers are bearer-only. Error envelopes carry debug only in development, unique violations map to bare `CONFLICT`, the reporter sees no body or headers. The config schema refuses in production every committed secret and seed password, `CORS_ORIGINS=*`, an unstated proxy hop, loopback or `http` app URLs, an unset mail transport, a short metrics token, and half a Google or SMTP pair. `.env.example` holds no real secret. `/metrics` compares its bearer in constant time. Prune runs as the application role and never echoes the connection string.
