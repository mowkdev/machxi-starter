## D15.4 — The admin resource screen, through the generator

**Built 2026-09-05.** No record moved at this head: every ADR the step names was already in force, and the generator is instruction 0 rather than a decision to accept.

**`pnpm turbo gen resource-screen organization organizations false` produced twenty-two files and ran the build**, which is the first time that generator has been asked for a real screen rather than a demo. It is worth recording what it got right and what it could not: it wrote the entity's keys, api, queries and config, the feature's two surfaces and three dialogs, the thin page, four routes, the MSW handlers, both catalogues seeded, the namespace and `CustomTypeOptions` wiring, and the two lists that load them — and it printed the three edits that are decisions rather than shapes: the policy row, the sidebar entry, and swapping the page's placeholder `capabilities` line for `useAllowedCapabilities`. All three were made here.

What it could not know is the resource's own shape, and reshaping that was most of the step: the template guesses `name` and `description`, where an organization has a name, a slug, a kind, a `createdBy` and a `deletedAt`, and where the **create body is not the update body** — `POST /admin/organizations` names an owner by id because staff are never members (ADR-0067 §3) and `PATCH` cannot move ownership at all. That asymmetry is carried by one line, `variants: ["create"]` on `ownerId`, rather than by two surfaces each remembering it.

**`assertConfigMatchesContract` earned its place.** One call reported five disagreements between the reshaped config and the endpoint, in one message: a `name` filter the allow-list did not have, a uuid the generic sampler could not guess, a `slug` likewise, `ownerId` on the edit variant, and two enum values with no `common:options` label. Four were the config's fault and one was the contract's — `name` joined `LIST_ORGANIZATIONS_FILTERABLE`, index-backed by the `(name, id)` pair the table already had.

**The record moved from a dialog to a page**, which is the step's other structural change. The members section is a table beside a form, and neither overlay can hold that: a dialog would put a scrolling table in a modal, a drawer in a column narrower than the grid it came from. `fullHeight` is computed from that same config line, so the route follows.

**The members section is the first record surface in this app with a sub-resource**, and it is deliberately not a second grid: `@repo/grid` is a screen's worth of machinery — URL state, preferences, a column control, selection, a pager — and a members list is one organization's handful of people. A second grid would also put list params in a URL ADR-0044 says carries only the record. Four operations, matching the API's four exactly.

**Two reviews, `code-reviewer` and `security-reviewer` (the D15 constraint). No BLOCKER. Seven findings, all seven taken; both reviewers found the first independently.**

1. **A failed members fetch rendered "This organization has no members."** The only read was `members.data?.items ?? []`, and queries are silent by default — so a `404` from a concurrently deleted organization, a `5xx` or a dropped connection all told an operator, with authority, that a tenant had nobody in it, and then offered **Add member** over that false picture. It is the shape ADR-0042 names as worse than the defect it was written to remove. The query now carries `meta: { errorToast: true }` and the table branches on `isError`.
2. **The delete mock was more permissive than the service, on exactly the refusal the copy promises.** The screen's own strings say twice that a personal organization is refused, the fixtures ship one, and the handler deleted it — and hard-deleted, where the API soft-deletes and keeps answering `200` for the row. Both corrected.
3. **Nothing on the screen could reach a soft-deleted organization.** The `deletedAt` column was declared with a comment saying the users screen had dropped this control at `D4.6` — it had dropped the _column's_ filter icon and kept the toolbar stance. Without the stance the repository's default scope never steps aside, so a deleted tenant vanished from the only screen that lists tenants, with its memberships surviving and its slug released. The presence hot filter is back, with its catalogue key.
4. **The membership handlers bypassed the contract** — the list ignored `listMembershipsQuery` and the role change cast its body. Naming the input made it concrete: changing the section's sort to a column outside `LIST_MEMBERSHIPS_SORTABLE` would have kept the suite green and answered `VALIDATION_ERROR` in production. Both parse now.
5. **The manager-Delete assertion could not fail.** Row Delete is a `menuitem` in a closed dropdown, so `queryByRole("button", { name: "Delete" })` finds nothing for _any_ role. It now opens the menu, with an admin positive control beside it so a Delete that stopped rendering for everybody cannot leave it green.
6. **The personal-slug refusal landed at the top of the form** rather than under the box the comment promised: it carries `ORGANIZATION_PERSONAL_UNDELETABLE`, and `errorFields` mapped only `CONFLICT`. Mapped.
7. **Two member cases never asserted which member they acted on**, and six catalogue keys were dead — a `joined` column nothing renders, a `changeRole` dialog that is an inline `Select`, and a `kind` block the badge resolves through `common:options` instead. Assertions tightened, keys deleted in both locales.

One note taken as prose rather than a change: `memberLists(id)` sits **under** `detail(id)`, so an organization's own `PATCH` refetches the members table. Harmless, and the header no longer reads "nothing else" as a claim about both directions.

**Gate green** — `lint` 17/17, `check-types` 16/16, `test` 15/15 (admin 56 files, 497 tests), `size` 4/4, `format:check` clean — plus `pnpm test:scripts` 28/28 and `node scripts/docs-hygiene.mjs` clean over 11 specs. **No Postman change**: this step adds no endpoint, and `D15.3` covered the ones it drives.

**The spec gained one clause**, on the admin list's filterable fields — the one API behaviour this step changed.

**Worth knowing before `D15.5`.** The admin bundle is at **345.3 kB of a 353.5 kB ceiling** — 8 kB of headroom, and this step spent a screen's worth. The next admin screen will want the budget raised or the chunking revisited, and that is a decision rather than a bump. Three things the security review named as residual and did not require: a sub-resource sharing its parent's policy key is a new transcription hazard, since `organizations.update` now stands for four independently-guarded routes and the table's shape has nowhere to show a divergence; the members list is a fixed `limit: 50` with no pager, so a large organization truncates silently and "who is the last owner" is computed from a partial page; and a personal organization's slug — which carries its owner's account id — can travel in a copied admin URL through `q` or the slug filter, which is within ADR-0067 §2's staff-only bound but is a wider audience than the record page alone.

## D15.3 — The API surface, both segments, the exclusions, Postman

**Built 2026-09-05.** [ADR-0015](../adr/0015-route-segments-and-versioning.md) and [ADR-0057](../adr/0057-impersonation-sessions.md) moved to `superseded by` [ADR-0067](../adr/0067-organizations.md) at the head — 0015 for a prefix _within_ the `user` segment (`/orgs/:orgId`; the three segments, their guards and the version stand) and 0057 for §5's list alone (the mint, the handoff, the actor claim, the fixed session life and the attribution stand). 0057 is the fifth record here to carry more than one partial successor, and **all four of 0067's parents have now moved**.

Built: `@repo/contracts/organizations` — three resources, the params and bodies, and the three list allow-lists; the module's mapper, schemas, three controllers and three routers; the service's whole HTTP surface; the repositories' list surfaces; `ORGANIZATION_INVITATION_TTL` through the config schema and `.env.example`; a pending invitation in the dev fixtures whose token is minted per run; three Postman folders with their environment variables; and three integration suites plus the list-index tests.

Nineteen endpoints across three mounts. `/user/orgs` — the switcher's list and a team create outside the prefix, then get, patch, delete, `/members` (list, role, remove-or-leave) and `/invitations` (create, list, revoke) under it, each route naming the membership roles it admits. `/user/invitations` — the invitee's list, accept and decline, self-scoped by the caller's **address**, which is read from their account because no principal carries one. `/admin/organizations` — the users resource's shape exactly, `admin` or `manager` to read with every write narrowed to `admin`, plus a memberships sub-resource that adds by user id.

**Two reviews, `code-reviewer` and `security-reviewer` (the D15 constraint). No BLOCKER. Nine findings, all nine taken.**

1. **HIGH — a second member could be put into a `personal` organization**, from either segment: the caller holds `owner` on their own, so `requireMembership("owner","admin")` admits them, and staff reach the same row by id. ADR-0067 §2 bounds the accepted "a uuid and a creation timestamp in a URL" exposure precisely with _"only its owner and staff ever see the slug, and no surface a second party reads may carry one"_ — and this step built that surface. Worse followed: ownership could then be granted away, so `findPersonalFor`'s `created_by` key and the derived slug would name different accounts and `ensurePersonalOrganizationFor`'s idempotence proof would fail; and the creator closing their account soft-deletes the organization, silently evicting anybody else. Both paths now refuse `409 ORGANIZATION_PERSONAL_CLOSED`. **The invariant was a fact of the record with nothing in the code holding it up.**
2. **MEDIUM — an address could be invited without bound.** An invitation has no natural key, so nothing in the schema stops a second one while the first is outstanding, and the cost lands on somebody else: the invitee's own list is deliberately unpaged, so an unrelated account's list could be made arbitrarily long from an address the inviter never controlled — and the day the mail deliverable fills §2's marked call, the same loop is a mail bomb at a third party. One live offer per address per organization now, `409 ORGANIZATION_INVITATION_PENDING`, revocable and then repeatable.
3. **`findByEmail` counted a closed account's membership**, so an address could become permanently un-invitable: team memberships survive a soft delete while `users_email_active_unique` releases the address, so the dead row would match by email forever and the owner would be told "already a member" about somebody who is not. It now filters `deleted_at`, the same clause `countOwnersExcept` carries.
4. **`GET /admin/organizations/:id/memberships` answered `200` with an empty page** for an id nothing holds, while `GET /:id` on the same prefix answers `404` — nothing on that segment having proved the organization exists. It reads first now, through a `listMembersForStaff` distinct from the user segment's, which is already behind the guard.
5. **`acceptInvitation` read the organization and the membership after its transaction committed**, so a concurrent removal could produce a `200` reporting `role: null` for a membership the same request had just created. Both reads moved inside.
6. **The dev seeder's "already pending" probe omitted the expiry**, so a re-seed a month later would find the expired row, skip the insert, and leave the invitee's surface with nothing to answer.
7. Three smaller ones: `inviteeAccessToken` was a `default` Postman variable where every other token in the file is `secret`; `callerAccount` answered "Organization not found" on routes that name no organization; and the `get` handler was typed against the patch schema.
8. **A dead test and a dead script.** The impersonation role-grant case invited somebody and never accepted, then granted against the _owner's_ own membership — an operation the last-owner invariant would refuse anyway, so the case did not exercise what it was named for. It now brings a real second member in and promotes them to `owner`, with the account's own owner doing the same thing successfully afterwards. A `qaInviteNonce` prerequest that nothing read was removed.

**Two notes taken as written rather than fixed.** The members list's default sort (`-createdAt` with an ascending tiebreaker) is a mixed-direction order no single btree scan serves — the repository-wide convention rather than this table's problem, and the comment that claimed the `ORDER BY` matched the composite now says what actually happens. And `GET /user/orgs` ships **without** ADR-0014's list language, against this step's own plan line: it is the switcher's query over a handful of rows, so a pagination language there would be a query string no client sends. That is a labelled amendment to the plan entry rather than a silent divergence.

**Gate green** — `lint` 17/17, `check-types` 16/16, `test` 15/15 (API 36 unit files, 24 integration), `size` 4/4, `format:check` clean — plus `pnpm test:scripts` 28/28, `node scripts/docs-hygiene.mjs` clean over 11 specs, `node scripts/rules-digest.mjs --check` up to date, and **`pnpm test:postman` 165 requests / 504 assertions / 0 failures** against the dev stack.

**The spec grew six requirements** ([`docs/specs/organizations.md`](../specs/organizations.md)): the caller's own organizations and the create; name and slug as the whole of an organization; the members list with removal-or-leaving; the invitation create and its three `409`s; the invitee's surface and its single use; the admin resource; and the impersonated session's refusals. Two new error codes entered the enum with all four dictionaries, which the apps' compile-time completeness check over `ErrorCode` made unskippable.

**Worth knowing before `D15.4`.** The Postman collection now spends about 160 of the 300 global requests per fifteen minutes, so it runs **once per window** against one API process where it used to run several times; the README says so, and the fix is to restart `pnpm dev --filter api` between passes. Two things the security review named as residual and did not require: an organization `admin` may remove `owner`s down to the last one — griefing rather than takeover, since only an owner grants roles, but §2 never decided whether `admin` outranks `owner` for removal; and the invitation **create** has no limiter class of its own, which wants deciding before the send exists (§5 already commits the future token _lookup_ to the login/refresh class). Both belong to the deliverable that ships the mail, and neither is a gap in what D15 promises.

## D15.2 — The module core, the guard, the context, the personal organization on every path

**Built 2026-09-04.** [ADR-0019](../adr/0019-authorization-guards.md) moved to `superseded by` [ADR-0067](../adr/0067-organizations.md) at the head — a delta: a third guard joins the two, and every rule 0019 makes about population, role, refinement-by-narrowing and the `401` for a wrong-population or anonymous caller stands. The index rows, the topic table, the ledger's window paragraph and 0067's `Supersedes:` line all moved with it.

Built: `apps/api/src/modules/organizations/` — the three repositories (`organizations`, `memberships`, `invitations`), the service, `requireMembership` and the module's error factories; `ctx.organization` on the request context, written by the guard and by nothing else; `ensurePersonalOrganizationFor` called inside `register`'s transaction and inside `POST /admin/users`'s, `deletePersonalOrganizationFor` inside both user-deletion transactions, each as a narrowed service-to-service dependency; the personal organization's name and slug derivation moved from `@repo/db/seeds` into `packages/db/src` so the API and the seeders share one expression; two domain error codes with their four dictionaries; the container wiring; and the tenancy line in the API digest grown with the guard and the deletion path.

**No amendment to ADR-0067 was needed** — the record's §2, §4 and §5 described what was built, which makes this the first D15 step that has not corrected its own record. The plan's `D15.3` entry took one, labelled, carrying two findings forward (below).

**Two reviews, `code-reviewer` and `security-reviewer` (the D15 constraint). No BLOCKER. Six findings, all six taken.**

1. **The one that mattered most, and the gate could not see it.** `invitations.listPendingForEmail` used the relational query builder with a **correlated** `exists` subquery. The relational builder aliases the table it selects from, so the subquery's reference resolved to nothing and Postgres refused the statement outright — `42P01`, every call, any input. Nothing called it yet (`GET /user/invitations` is `D15.3`'s), so all five links were green over a query that could never run. Confirmed by executing it before fixing it; rewritten as a core `select` with an `innerJoin`, the shape `memberships.listForUser` already used. **The general lesson is the second half of the fix**: this step builds a module core one step ahead of the routes that call it, so a new `apps/api/test/organizations-repositories.int.test.ts` now executes every repository function against real Postgres and asserts what the record fixes. It caught this and finding 2 on its first run.
2. **The last-owner invariant counted closed accounts as owners.** Both deletion paths leave an account's **team** memberships in place — only the personal organization goes with it — and `user_id` cascades on a hard delete only, so a soft-deleted account still held its `owner` row. Two-owner team, owner A closes their account, owner B leaves: the count saw A and allowed it, leaving an organization with no owner who can sign in, no path to grant one and no path to delete it. `countOwnersExcept` now joins `users` and filters `deleted_at is null`.
3. **The owner lock was taken after the read it was meant to cover.** `assertOwnerSurvives` locked only once a role read said `owner`, which leaves the non-owner path unserialised — and a non-owner path can still empty an organization: a removal reads `member`, skips the lock, and by the time it deletes, that member has been promoted and the previous sole owner removed by a request that counted this one as the survivor. Two writes, each correct against what it read, no owner left. The lock is now the first statement of both membership writes, in a `lockedMembershipWrite` helper, which is the ordering `staff.service.ts` had all along. The unit test that pinned the weaker ordering (`lockOwnerInvariant === 0` for a non-owner removal) asserted the bug as if it were the requirement, and now asserts the fix.
4. **Both new test fakes recorded calls nothing read**, so the claim their docstrings made was unproved at the unit tier. One assertion added per path: `register` and `POST /admin/users` ensure the organization, both deletion paths delete it with the account's own `at`.
5. **The tenant scope was spread-overridable** in `invitations.create` — the scope was spread over by `input`. Structural typing only excess-checks object literals, so a wider variable could have put an `organizationId` over the required parameter. The scope now goes last.
6. **The whole `UserRecord`, `passwordHash` included, was handed to the organizations service** for a call that needs four fields. The auth service already built the object by hand at its own call site; the users service now matches it.

Two smaller corrections went in with them: the "a duplicate leaves the organization count unchanged" tests carried a comment claiming they proved a rollback, when today the transaction returns before the organization is ever written — the comment now says what the assertion is really guarding against, and the assertion stays; and `organizations.findBySlug` gained a note that it carries no membership scope, because a slug is an existence oracle exactly as an id is.

**Gate green** — `lint` 17/17, `check-types` 16/16, `test` 15/15 (API 35 unit files, 22 integration), `size` 4/4, `format:check` clean — plus `pnpm test:scripts` 28/28, `node scripts/docs-hygiene.mjs` clean over 11 specs, and `node scripts/rules-digest.mjs --check` up to date.

**The spec grew five requirements** ([`docs/specs/organizations.md`](../specs/organizations.md)): the personal organization on both creation paths, its deletion with the account, the owner invariant including the closed-account case, the guard's two refusals, and a soft-deleted organization ceasing to be a tenant — which is where the memberships-and-invitations-survive rule now lives. `users-and-profile.md` and `auth-sessions.md` gained a clause each where their endpoints acquired the side effect, pointing at the organizations spec rather than restating it.

**Carried into `D15.3`, as a labelled amendment to its plan entry.** `PATCH /user/orgs/:orgId` must refuse a slug edit on a `personal` organization: "exactly one personal organization" is proved by the slug being derived from the account's id, and an editable slug takes the proof away. And its integration suite carries the closed-account owner case, which `D15.2` fixed in SQL but had no route to prove through — `organizations-repositories.int.test.ts` proves it at the repository tier in the meantime.

**Logged, not fixed.** `invitations.findById` and `listPendingFor` do not filter on the organization being live, unlike every other read in the module; they are safe only while their callers sit behind `requireMembership`, which resolves live organizations only — worth checking in `D15.3`'s review when those callers land. `ensurePersonalOrganizationFor` is idempotent by _slug_ but recovers by `createdBy` and `kind`, which agree only while a personal slug is immutable — the same fact the carried amendment above protects. And what happens to a team organization whose last owner closes their account is still unanswered: ADR-0067 §7 puts it outside this deliverable, and it wants a record of its own in the one that ships the members list.

## D15.1 — the operator's answer to the escalated finding (not a step)

Kept here rather than inline with `D15.1`'s own notes, which stay in the plan where they were written: this was written after [ADR-0068](../adr/0068-behaviour-specs-and-plan-notes.md) was accepted, and its §2 splits the notes from the plan from that acceptance forward.

**Answered 2026-09-04 by the operator, as its own commit and not as a step.** The token column, not the verified-address gate: `organization_invitations.token_hash` (`not null`, unique, SHA-256 of an opaque token — the `sessions.refresh_token_hash` shape) lands now, in migration `0013_invitation_token_hash`, while the window is open. It is **unused in D15** — acceptance still matches by address, since nothing here can deliver a token — and `D15.3` mints it into the row it creates, which is the whole of the change to what D15 does. Single use is the row's existing terminal stamps, not a fourth one. ADR-0067 §2 and §5 amended in place, labelled; §Consequences keeps the residual risk, now recorded as **accepted rather than fixed**. `D15.1` stays ticked and is not reopened.

**Logged by the token column's review, 2026-09-04.** `D15.3`: a Postman or dev fixture invitation must mint `sha256Base64url(randomToken())` and never a literal — a constant hash in a seed is one shared credential across every clone the day accept-by-token exists. Beyond D15, for the deliverable that ships the send: whether acceptance binds to the **bearer** of the token or to a verified address is the decision that actually closes this finding and deserves its own record; **resend and rotation** have no semantics yet (`not null` and unique mean a resend either re-sends the same token or rotates the hash); and **retention** — no sweep is planned, so a consumed or expired row keeps the invited address indefinitely, which is PII rather than a credential and wants the `db:sessions:prune` shape.
